ML feasibility & action plan

A staged path from high-quality labels to validated AI assistance—not autonomous surgical guidance.

Research / annotation support only
Recommended sequence
V1 focus: time and label quality
1

Data governance + label protocol · Weeks 0–2

Secure video rights, consent and institutional approvals; define 10–13 phase labels, boundary rules, instrument vocabulary, uncertainty codes, and reviewer workflow. Remove patient identifiers and restrict access.

2

Capture baseline · Weeks 2–6

Collect 50–100 representative cases with surgeon/site/device metadata. Double-annotate a stratified subset. Measure manual minutes per case, reviewer edits, agreement, and missing/ambiguous intervals before introducing AI.

3

Train a phase-proposal baseline · Weeks 5–9

Start with a pretrained image/video encoder sampled at 1–2 frames/sec. Pool frame embeddings and train a temporal convolutional network (MS-TCN) or light Transformer head for frame-wise phase labels. Add transition smoothing / duration constraints; surface confidence and defer low-confidence spans.

4

Instrument presence proposals · Weeks 8–12

First ship time intervals or multi-label presence tags (e.g., phaco probe, I/A handpiece, cannula, IOL injector). Use a pretrained detector / image classifier with human correction. Only add pixel masks after label volume, consent, and clinical value justify the cost.

5

Prospective pilot + gated release · Weeks 12–16

Run in shadow mode, compare with independent ophthalmologist labels, and measure annotation-time reduction without lowering label quality. Keep a human approval requirement; do not present outputs as intraoperative advice.

Feasibility verdict: A useful annotation-product MVP and a pilot phase model are feasible. 50–100 local surgeries can validate workflow and produce a small, domain-specific baseline; they are not enough to claim robust cross-surgeon/site generalization or to train reliable anatomical segmentation from scratch.
Practical model stack
Video → frame sampling (1–2 fps for phase; higher for short transitions)

Pretrained visual encoder (VideoMAE / TimeSformer / ImageNet backbone, compare rather than assume)
↓
Temporal head (MS-TCN first; Transformer as benchmark)
↓
Calibrated phase probabilities → merge spans, preserve uncertainty

Annotator correction + provenance log
Evaluation gates
  • Split by patient and surgeon; hold out a center when available.
  • Macro-F1 / per-phase recall and segmental F1 at temporal IoU thresholds; include boundary error.
  • Calibration, abstention rate, and AI suggestions accepted / edited / rejected.
  • Human outcome: annotation minutes saved with non-inferior reviewer agreement.
  • Report confidence intervals; do not rely on accuracy alone with imbalanced phases.
Risks to resolve early
  • Video ownership, consent, ethics review, privacy, retention, and permitted reuse.
  • Domain shift from microscope, camera, compression, surgeon style, and procedure variation.
  • Rare/short phases and label-boundary disagreement; explicit adjudication rules.
  • Dataset licensing and access conditions for public benchmark data.
Evidence from the supplied papers
  • Cataract-1K (Scientific Data, 2024): 1,000 videos overall; phase labels are reported for 56 regular videos (13 phases); pixel-level annotations cover selected frames from 30 videos. [DOI: 10.1038/s41597-024-03193-4]
  • Cataract-LMM (Scientific Data, 2026): 3,000 videos from two centers; phase labels for 150 videos across 13 phases; instance masks on selected frames and interaction tracking on a subset. [DOI: 10.1038/s41597-026-07464-0]

These paper-reported dataset sizes are not a guarantee that data are available for your intended use. Verify access, license, ethics, and permitted commercial use before training.
